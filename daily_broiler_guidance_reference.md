# Daily broiler guidance reference

The dashboard guidance is a concise operational checklist, not a diagnosis or substitute for a veterinarian or poultry-production specialist. It is intentionally synchronized to age bands because young chicks need closer brooding and access checks, while all ages require bird-level environmental observation.

## Battery-cage adaptation

The current application uses a battery-cage housing model. The guidance therefore treats the **cage row** as the daily inspection unit, rather than using floor-litter management as a proxy. It focuses on even access to trough/feed lines and drinker lines, bird behaviour and breathing at cage level, heat and airflow at bird level, drops/manure collection below cages, and the condition of rows, cages, and equipment. This is an operational adaptation of established poultry feed/water, environment, and manure-management principles; specific equipment timing and cleaning procedures must follow the cage manufacturer’s instructions and the farm’s veterinary program.

| Age band | Guidance themes used in the application | Evidence basis |
|---|---|---|
| Days 1–3 | Immediate feed and water access, crop-fill observation, bird-level temperature/humidity checks, no drafts, and behaviour-based adjustment. | Aviagen-derived chick-management guidance states that temperature and RH should be recorded at least twice daily during the first five days, recommends 60–70% RH for the first three days, and emphasizes bird behaviour, minimum ventilation, and rapid access to feed and water. [1] |
| Days 4–7 | Check uniform feed and water access, maintain litter and drinker hygiene, record environmental conditions, and review day-seven weight/uniformity. | Aviagen-derived guidance describes observing feed/water access, verifying the early start using day-seven weight and uniformity, and monitoring RH and temperature at chick level. [1] |
| Days 8–21 | Observe distribution, respiratory comfort, litter quality, drinker flow, feed access, and ventilation without chilling the birds. | Poultry Hub explains that microclimate, temperature, RH, air composition, and air movement should be measured at bird level, and that animal behaviour is the best indicator of temperature comfort. [2] |
| Days 22+ | Manage heat load, water availability, airflow, litter moisture, and daily behaviour/respiratory observation; escalate unusual mortality or clinical signs. | Poultry Hub notes that high temperature reduces feed intake and that higher air velocity can assist cooling at warmer temperatures, while high humidity can contribute to wet litter and micro-organism growth. [2] |

For cage systems, Mississippi State University Extension describes manure handling as a key management dimension in cage housing and notes its relevance to ammonia emissions and air quality. [3]

## Dashboard verification

The dashboard was verified at the actual placement date, showing the **بداية التحضين** guidance for cycle day 1. Changing the selected date to 24 August 2026 updated the dashboard to cycle day 7 and replaced the card with **استكمال التحضين** guidance, including the day-seven weight-sampling reminder. The same selected date also updated weather context, environmental targets, expected live weight, and consumption forecasts.

After the battery-cage rewrite, the day-one card was again checked in the live preview. It explicitly directs the operator to inspect cage rows, trough/feed access, drinker or nipple-line flow, behaviour within the cage, cage-level environment, and droppings collection below the row; it does not prescribe floor-litter management.

Changing the selected cycle date to day 7 was also verified. The card changed to **استكمال التحضين**, retained the cage-row context, and specifically listed row-by-row water-line checks, cage-level behaviour, and cleanliness of manure channels or belts.
